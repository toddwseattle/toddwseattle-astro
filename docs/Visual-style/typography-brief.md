# Typography Brief: Editorial, Durable, Quiet

## Typography Intent

Typography should:

- Disappear during reading
- Feel academic and reflective
- Support essays, notes, and teaching material

The site should feel closer to a **book or journal** than a product UI.

---

## Primary Principles

1. **Readability First**
   - Long-form reading is the primary use case
   - Comfort > expressiveness

2. **Low Contrast Hierarchy**
   - Headings differ by size and tone, not color
   - Avoid dramatic weight jumps

3. **Timeless Over Trendy**
   - Avoid fashionable display fonts
   - Choose faces that age well

---

## Font Roles

### Body Text

- Preferred: Newsreader
- Serif or humanist sans fallback
- Warm tone preferred
- Designed for paragraphs, not UI labels

Examples (non-prescriptive):

- Newsreader
- Source Serif
- Literata
- Charter
- IBM Plex Serif
- Humanist sans if serif is avoided

### Headings

- Preferred: Newsreader
- Same family as body, or closely related
- Slightly darker color
- Slightly tighter tracking

### UI and Data Labels

- Preferred: Manrope or Public Sans
- Used for buttons, filters, metadata, timeline labels, and compact controls
- Provides clear distinction from narrative content
- Must remain quiet and functional, not branded or expressive

### Monospace

- Neutral, low-contrast
- Used only for code and technical notation

---

## Layout Rules

- Line length: 60–75 characters
- Line height: 1.6–1.75
- Paragraph spacing > indentation
- Avoid justified text
- Avoid all-caps except for metadata

---

## What Typography Should NOT Do

- Signal branding through novelty
- Compete with content
- Look “designed” at first glance

If someone notices the font before the idea, it is wrong.

---

## Success Criteria

Typography is successful if:

- Reading feels effortless
- Pages resemble printed material
- Visual consistency survives theme switching
